Interview

"Tell me about yourself" — best answer with samples

Updated November 2026 · RDW Careers

"Tell me about yourself" is the first question in almost every interview. Interviewers use it to judge your communication, confidence and whether your background fits the role. A clear 60-second answer sets the tone for the whole interview.

The formula: Present → Past → Future

  1. Present — who you are now (degree or current role) in one line.
  2. Past — two proofs: a project, internship or achievement with a result.
  3. Future — why this role is your next step.

Sample answer for freshers

"I'm Ravi, a 2026 B.Tech Computer Science graduate from Nellore. I enjoy working with data — in my final-year project I built a dashboard in Power BI that cut our department's weekly report time from two hours to ten minutes. During my internship at a local retail company I cleaned and analysed around 12,000 customer records in SQL and Excel. I'm looking to start my career as a data analyst, and this role interests me because of your team's work in retail analytics."

Sample answer for experienced candidates

"I'm a Java backend developer with four years of experience, currently at a fintech company where I build payment APIs used by about two lakh customers a day. Recently I reduced our API response time by 35% by redesigning the caching layer, and I mentor two junior developers. I'm now looking for a role where I can work on larger-scale systems, which is why this position stood out to me."

Mistakes to avoid

  • Repeating your whole resume line by line.
  • Family details, hobbies or your life story — unless asked.
  • Memorised lines delivered too fast.
  • Going beyond 90 seconds.

How to practise

Write your answer, then speak it aloud five times with a timer — aim for 50–70 seconds. Record yourself once on your phone and fix anything that sounds unnatural.

FAQ

How long should "Tell me about yourself" be?

About 60 seconds — between 50 and 90 seconds is ideal.

Can I mention my hometown and family?

One line about where you are from is fine. Skip family details unless the interviewer asks.
